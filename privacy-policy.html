Privacy Policy — Expense Tracker

Last updated: 1 November 2026

Expense Tracker is a private, offline-first app for tracking expenses, budgets, debts and credit cards. Everything you enter stays on your own device. The app has no accounts, no servers and no analytics, and it does not need an internet connection. We have no way to see your data.

What we collect

Nothing. The developer collects, receives and stores no personal data, no usage data and no analytics. There is no backend, no account system, no advertising SDK and no third-party tracking in this app.

What the app stores on your device

Everything you enter is kept in a private on-device database:

  • Expenses, income, categories, budgets and recurring rules
  • Debts you owe and are owed
  • Credit cards you add, and their statements and bill payments
  • Your settings: name, currency, theme and reminder preferences

About credit cards

The app stores only what is needed to track a bill: a card name you choose, the bank or issuer, the credit limit, the statement and due dates, and optionally the last four digits as a label. It never asks for and never stores a full card number, CVV, PIN, expiry date or banking credentials. The app cannot connect to your bank — every figure is one you entered yourself.

Permissions

  • Notifications — used only for reminders your device schedules locally: expense logging nudges and credit card bill reminders. Declining it disables reminders and nothing else.
  • Biometric / screen lock — if you turn on App Lock, the app asks Android to verify you with the fingerprint, face or screen lock you already use. That happens entirely inside the operating system. The app never sees or stores your fingerprint, face data or PIN; it only learns whether Android approved the attempt.

Android also lists a few permissions that the app's scheduling library adds and that are granted automatically, without a prompt. None of them can read your data:

  • Run at startup — re-arms reminders after the phone restarts, so a bill reminder isn't lost by rebooting.
  • Prevent sleeping and foreground service — let a scheduled reminder finish reliably.
  • View network connections — part of the same library, and never used, because there is nothing to connect to.

Most importantly, the app does not hold the INTERNET permission. Android will not let it open a network connection at all, so it is not technically capable of transmitting your data anywhere — by design, rather than by promise.

Backups — the one case where data leaves the device

Your data can leave the device only by your own action, or through Android's own backup feature. We never receive a copy either way.

  • Android Auto Backup. Like most Android apps, this app lets the operating system include its data in your device backup. Android encrypts that backup and stores it in your Google account, so your expenses survive a new phone. That is handled by Google and your device, not by us, and is covered by Google's privacy policy. You can switch it off in Android Settings → Google → Backup.
  • Backup file (JSON). Settings → Backup writes a file to a location you pick. If you enable weekly automatic backups, the app writes to the folder you chose — including a cloud folder, if that is what you picked.
  • CSV export. Settings → Export writes a CSV you can save or share.

Files you export or back up are yours to manage; once shared, their privacy depends on where you put them.

Your control over your data

  • Export everything at any time as CSV or as a full JSON backup.
  • Delete everything by uninstalling the app, which removes its local database. There is no server-side copy, because none exists.

Children's privacy

The app is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, of any age.

Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices change — for example if cloud sync or advertising were ever added — this policy will be updated and the date revised before that version is released.
